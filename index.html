A fence you cannot see, watched all night.

GeoRaksha draws invisible fences on a map — around a yard, a depot, a site gate — and raises an alert the moment a vehicle or a person crosses one, or stops reporting altogether.

The problem

Boundaries exist. Watching them does not.

Every yard, depot and site has a boundary. What it rarely has is a pair of eyes on that boundary at 2 a.m.

01

Fences on paper

A site boundary usually lives in a register, or in a guard's head. Nobody can watch every gate, all night, without blinking — and nobody should have to.

02

Crossings nobody sees

A truck leaves the yard at 2 a.m. The log book finds out at 9. By then the question is not where it went, but why nobody knew when it left.

03

Silence looks like safety

A tracker that stops reporting looks exactly like one that is standing still. The gap is only noticed when someone goes looking for it.

The solution

How GeoRaksha watches

Three steps, and no step is a person watching a screen.

Step 1

Draw the fence

Draw a polygon on the map, or drop a circle with a radius. That is the whole setup — no code, no coordinates typed by hand.

Step 2

The engine checks every report

Each position report is tested against every fence with real geometry — point-in-polygon for drawn zones, true distance for circles. Not estimates.

Step 3

Alerts the moment it happens

Enter, exit, silence, recovered. The alert is raised by the same event that crossed the line — not by a summary written at the end of a shift.
